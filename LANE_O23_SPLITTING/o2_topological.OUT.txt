========================================================================================================
WHAT PART 2 ESTABLISHES
========================================================================================================
  The vanishing of the first-order matrix element in the topological case is NOT a genericity
  statement and NOT an accident of the perturbation chosen.  It is the Knill-Laflamme condition:
  every Pauli of weight below the distance either anticommutes with a stabiliser (then PVP = 0) or
  lies in the stabiliser group (then PVP = +-P).  Both are SCALAR, so Phi vanishes IDENTICALLY on the
  whole space of sub-distance perturbations -- rank Phi = 0, not 'rank Phi generically small'.

  THE ONE-SENTENCE CONTRAST WITH THE 0-FORM CASE:
     a 0-form charge is a SUM OF LOCAL DENSITIES, so some density is already non-scalar on the
     multiplet and rank Phi >= 1 at weight 1;
     a 1-form charge is carried by NON-CONTRACTIBLE LOOPS, which are not sums of local densities, so
     rank Phi = 0 until the weight reaches the length of the shortest such loop, which is d.
  The exponent d in Delta ~ eps^d is the LENGTH OF THE SHORTEST NON-SCALAR OPERATOR.  For a 0-form
  symmetry that length is 1.
