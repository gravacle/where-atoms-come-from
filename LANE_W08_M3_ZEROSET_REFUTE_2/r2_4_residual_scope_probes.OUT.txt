(b) M3-F12's MISSING STEP, SUPPLIED (the lane flagged it 'numerically exact, structurally
    unproved').  Off the firing region one branch dominates everywhere.  |p00+p10 e^{it}|^2
    = p00^2+p10^2+2 p00 p10 cos t and |p01+p11 e^{it}|^2 are BOTH INCREASING IN cos t, so
    pointwise domination is equivalent to domination at the two ENDPOINTS cos t = +-1:
        p00+p10 > p01+p11   and   |p00-p10| > |p01-p11| .
    Adding these gives 2 max(p00,p10) > 2 max(p01,p11).  So the dominating branch carries
    the global maximum weight, and m(P) = m(p00+p10 x) = log max(p00,p10) = log p_max.  QED.
    SCOPE LIMIT OF THE STEP, and it is a real one: the argument uses that a TWO-TERM Mahler
    measure is log of the larger coefficient.  With a THIRD loop the dominating branch is a
    FOUR-term 2-variable polynomial and the step becomes 'the dominating branch is itself

(c) 'THE INVOLUTION IS STRONGER THAN THE MULTISET THEOREM' (M3-F10) -- THEY ARE INCOMPARABLE.
    W-03's involution sigma: 00<->11 and 10<->01 simultaneously.  Z_k * conj(uv)^k sends
    chi_c -> conj(chi_{sigma(c)}), so |Z_k(p)| = |Z_k(sigma p)| POINTWISE in k.  That is a
    Z_2 statement.  W-03's multiset theorem is an S_4 statement that holds only for lambda.
    DIRECTION 1 -- the involution does NOT give the multiset theorem:
      2000 random (p,f,c): max ||Z_1(p)| - |Z_1(00<->10 p)||           = 7.921e-01  (NOT a symmetry)
      the SAME 2000 p:     max |lambda(p) - lambda(00<->10 p)|          = 3.331e-16  (IS a symmetry)
    DIRECTION 2 -- the multiset theorem does NOT give the involution: lambda is an integral,
    and S_4-invariance of an integral says nothing pointwise in k.  The involution's
    pointwise content at k = 1 is exactly the quantity that is 7.4e-01 in the row above for
    a NON-sigma transposition.
    => neither implies the other.  'STRONGER' is the wrong relation; the right one is that
       sigma is the unique transposition-pair whose invariance is pointwise.
