THE OPERATIONALISATION I USE, AND IT IS DELIBERATELY STRICTER THAN THE LANE'S.
The lane diffs its arms at the INPUT bytes (sha256 of the weight vector) and asserts the hashes
are pairwise distinct.  THAT TEST CANNOT DETECT THE DEFECT IT IS AIMED AT.  Two arms with
distinct inputs can be identical in output by a symmetry -- and this corpus OWNS the symmetry:
W-03's involution 00<->11, 10<->01 multiplies Z_k by conj(u)^k conj(v)^k, leaves |Z_k| FIXED at
every k and every connection, and W-09 used it to correct W-08's naming (REGISTER:948-955).
So I diff at the OUTPUT bytes: for every arm of every comparison in the lane I compute the
float64 |Z_k| sequence itself, hash it, and count how many arms collapse to one datum.

MY CARRIERS ARE BUILT FRESH AND UNDER DIFFERENT CONVENTIONS FROM THE LANE'S:
  - d1[v,e] = -1 at head, +1 at tail (the OPPOSITE sign convention to the lane's and to S1's).
    Ranks and Betti numbers are sign-convention-independent; using the opposite one is a check
    that no result of mine depends on the convention.
  - B0b vertices indexed (i,j) -> 3*i + j  (the lane uses 3*j + i), so my vertex labels are a
    transpose of the lane's and my incidence matrices are NOT row-permutations of the lane's
    by the identity.
  - B4 built as an explicit vertex/edge/face list, independently of the lane's.
  - Ranks over Q by my own Fraction elimination.
