# Alpha nonselection and same-sector inheritance

This lane answers one narrowly typed question:

> If record formation does not select the electromagnetic fine-structure
> constant across possible parent models, why does every record independently
> traced to our realized visible electromagnetic sector nevertheless use the
> same alpha?

The answer is a pair of compatible exact results:

1. the existing RFT axioms do **not** select a unique electromagnetic alpha
   across their model class; and
2. after one connected electromagnetic sector and its action are fixed, every
   record constituted as a subsystem of that sector inherits the same base
   coupling and RG ancestry.

In short:

\[
 \text{not fixed across RFT models}
 \quad\text{but}\quad
 \text{fixed within one selected same-sector parent}.
\]

This proves the precise content of “once nature realized one electromagnetic
sector, its alpha became the common standard.”  It does not derive the observed
number, prove why that sector was realized, or prove that records performed the
initial selection.

It also yields a genuine **actual-world alpha theorem**.  Measurement anchors
the visible-sector value in our universe; same-sector inheritance then proves
that every actual record physically constituted from that visible sector must
use the same value after common RG/context transport.  Possible universes with
other values do not weaken this actual-world result.  What they prevent is only
a parameter-free derivation of the observed numeral from bare RFT.  The
canonical-\(U(1)\) cavity construction now proves that finite active-EM RFT
recordhood admits more than one alpha.  Whether more than one complete
matter/gravity universe does so remains a separate open question; a deeper
numerical derivation is not presumed to exist.

Equivalently, the theorem has an `ALLOW/REQUIRE/SELECT` ladder: finite RFT
recordhood **allows** a non-singleton alpha set; same-sector embedding in one
realized parent **requires** that parent's one running trajectory; complete-
universe numerical requirement is open; and a dynamical parent-selection law is
separate and may not exist.
