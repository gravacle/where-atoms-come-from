     READ 1 (FLATNESS) -- SUSTAINED.  A plateau means the curve does not move.  Lane A's rule-A
     plateau has total variation 0.000000000 across its 4 points.  The rule-B window lane A calls
     a plateau moves by 0.1649 across its 5 in-band sizes, with a largest single step of
     0.0675, and its longest genuinely flat run (step < 0.01) is 1 size(s).
     Rule B produces a MONOTONE CROSSING of the band, not a plateau -- the same shape the sealed
     T1 null was rejected for.  Lane A's headline 'flat to 1e-9 across 10%-90% of the environment'
     is a rule-A statement ONLY; rule B does not reproduce flatness on any carrier tested.

     READ 2 (MATCHED CONTROL) -- THIS TEST WENT AGAINST THE OBJECTION AND IS REPORTED AS SUCH.
     prism7 (V,L,C,dim_phys,min degree all identical to heawood; d=3 not 5) scores
     2 in-band sizes against heawood's 5.  So rule B is NOT purely an environment-size
     counter: at matched L it does order the two carriers the way d does.  The objection that
     rule B is a pure artefact of |E| is REFUTED by this arm and I do not score it.

     READ 3 (WHAT RULE B ACTUALLY TRACKS).  Its count moves with BOTH d and L:
       d=5 fixed, L=9   -> in-band 3
       d=5 fixed, L=12  -> in-band 4
       d=5 fixed, L=21  -> in-band 5
       d=3 fixed, L=12  -> in-band 1
       d=3 fixed, L=21  -> in-band 2
     A bar of '>= 4 in-band sizes' is therefore not a carrier-independent criterion: it can be
     met by raising L at fixed d.  Under rule B the corrected floor for 4 points is tri_chain12
     at L = 12 (in-band 4), not heawood at L = 21; dbl_chain9 at L = 9 scores 3, because an
     8-link environment has too few sizes to resolve four of them.  RULE A FLOOR = 9, RULE B FLOOR = 12.
     Both are below 21 and the disagreement between them is itself a rule dependence.
