--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A.  EVERY +-1-VALUED FUNCTIONAL, COUNTED.  NOT A SAMPLE -- THE WHOLE CLASS.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A functional f : {+-1}^m -> {+1,-1} is a vector of 2^m signs, so there are 2^(2^m) of them:
  16 at m=2, 256 at m=3, 65536 at m=4.  Every one is enumerated and classified on three axes:
     INVARIANT      f(g.s) = f(s) for all g in G_W
     MEAN ZERO      sum over all configurations of f(s) is exactly 0
     WRITER-ODD     f(g.s) = -f(s) for SOME g in G_W  (the theorem candidate's hypothesis)

    m  #functionals  invariant  non-invariant  writer-ODD  mean = 0  NON-INVARIANT AND MEAN != 0  fraction
    2            16          2             14           6         6                            8       1/2
    3           256          2            254          70        70                          184     23/32
    4         65536          2          65534        2790     12870                        52664 6583/8192

  READ, and it is the decisive number of Part 1:
  at m = 4 there are 65534 NON-INVARIANT functionals; only 12870 of them have mean zero.
  52664 of them -- 80.4% -- are NON-INVARIANT AND HAVE NON-ZERO MEAN.

  ==> THE THEOREM CANDIDATE'S THIRD LINE IS FALSE.  NON-INVARIANCE DOES NOT IMPLY WRITER-ODD,
      AND IT DOES NOT IMPLY MEAN ZERO.  This is a REFUTATION BY COMPLETE ENUMERATION, not by a
      failed search: the class was exhausted.

  And WRITER-ODD is a strictly stronger condition than mean-zero: at m = 4, 2790 functionals
  are writer-odd, 12870 have mean zero.  Every writer-odd one has mean zero (the implication
  that IS true), but the converse fails on 10080 functionals.

  THE SMALLEST EXPLICIT COUNTEREXAMPLES, exhibited rather than counted:
    m=2  f = (1 + s1)/2             values [Fraction(1, 1), Fraction(0, 1), Fraction(1, 1), Fraction(0, 1)] invariant False  writer-odd False  EXACT mean 1/2     
    m=2  f = s1                     values [1, -1, 1, -1]                               invariant False  writer-odd True   EXACT mean 0       
    m=2  f = 1                      values [1, 1, 1, 1]                                 invariant True   writer-odd False  EXACT mean 1       
    m=2  f = s1 + s2 + s1s2         values [3, -1, -1, -1]                              invariant False  writer-odd False  EXACT mean 0

  f = (1+s1)/2 -- 'record 1 reads +1' -- is NON-INVARIANT (the writer of record 1 changes it),
  is NOT writer-odd, and has EXACT mean 1/2.  It does NOT cancel.  One line kills the candidate.
  f = s1 + s2 + s1s2 has EXACT mean 0 and is odd under NO writer: mean-zero does not require
  writer-oddness either.  The two conditions are genuinely distinct, in both directions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  B.  THE EXACT CONDITION THAT REPLACES IT, AND ITS PROOF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Because G_W = (Z_2)^m acts simply transitively, the space of functionals is the REGULAR
  representation.  It decomposes into the 2^m one-dimensional characters chi_S, and every
  functional has a unique exact expansion  f = sum_S fhat(S) chi_S.  Then, identically,

        mean of f over the uniform measure  =  fhat(EMPTY SET) .

  EXACT CONDITION.   mean(f) = 0  <=>  fhat(EMPTY) = 0  <=>  f is ORTHOGONAL TO THE CONSTANTS
                                    <=>  f lies in the sum of the NON-TRIVIAL isotypic components.

  Proof: chi_EMPTY = 1, and the characters are orthogonal, so fhat(EMPTY) = <f,1> = mean(f).
  The group action enters ONLY through the fact that chi_S for S non-empty has mean 0, which
  holds because such a chi_S is negated by any writer that flips an odd number of the records
  in S -- and simple transitivity guarantees such a writer EXISTS.

  RELATIONS AMONG THE THREE CONDITIONS, verified by the complete enumeration above:
        writer-odd  ==>  mean zero  ==>  non-invariant (for non-constant f)
        mean zero   =/=>  writer-odd            (10080 counterexamples at m=4)
        non-invariant =/=> mean zero            (52664 counterexamples at m=4)

  VERIFICATION that fhat(EMPTY) is the mean, on exact random rational functionals:
      m  trial   sum_s f(s) / 2^m (exact)    fhat(EMPTY) (exact)  equal?
      2      0                      -5/16                  -5/16    True
      2      1                      65/48                  65/48    True
      3      0                      -1/16                  -1/16    True
      3      1                     -13/24                 -13/24    True
      4      0                   -215/192               -215/192    True
      4      1                      -1/64                  -1/64    True
      5      0                    -57/128                -57/128    True
      5      1                       5/16                   5/16    True

  Exact rational agreement in every case.  The transform is the mean; no floating point involved.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  C.  IS MEAN-ZERO A CONSEQUENCE OF THE GROUP, OR AN ARTIFACT OF THE UNIFORM MEASURE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  BOTH ARE NEEDED, and the exact statement separating them is this:

    (1) On a G-TORSOR -- a set on which G acts simply transitively -- the UNIFORM measure is the
        UNIQUE G-invariant probability measure.  (If mu(g.s) = mu(s) for all g and the action is
        transitive, mu is constant; normalisation fixes the constant.)
    (2) Under ANY G-invariant measure, the average of f equals the average of its INVARIANT part.
        On a torsor the invariant part is the constant fhat(EMPTY), so the mean is fhat(EMPTY).

  So mean-zero for the non-trivial isotypic components is an EXACT CONSEQUENCE OF THE GROUP
  ACTION, PROVIDED THE MEASURE IS REQUIRED TO BE WRITER-INVARIANT -- and on the torus that
  requirement pins the measure down to exactly one, the uniform one.  Drop writer-invariance of
  the measure and NOTHING survives: the mean of chi_S becomes whatever the measure says.

  Verified exactly.  Uniqueness of the invariant measure, by exact linear algebra on the actual
  action (D-15: the same solver on the two non-transitive controls, which have MANY invariant
  measures):

  carrier / writer group                         m  #orbits  #invariant probability measures  unique?
  TORUS  G_W = (Z_2)^m, simply transitive        2        1            EXACTLY ONE (uniform)     True
  CTRL-1 chain shape: one global flip            2        2             a 1-parameter family    False
  CTRL-2 writers reach only m-1 records          2        2             a 1-parameter family    False
  TORUS  G_W = (Z_2)^m, simply transitive        4        1            EXACTLY ONE (uniform)     True
  CTRL-1 chain shape: one global flip            4        8             a 7-parameter family    False
  CTRL-2 writers reach only m-1 records          4        2             a 1-parameter family    False
  TORUS  G_W = (Z_2)^m, simply transitive        6        1            EXACTLY ONE (uniform)     True
  CTRL-1 chain shape: one global flip            6       32            a 31-parameter family    False
  CTRL-2 writers reach only m-1 records          6        2             a 1-parameter family    False

  READ: on the torus there is EXACTLY ONE writer-invariant measure at every m, so 'uniform' is
  not a modelling choice -- it is the unique measure compatible with the writer symmetry.  On
  CTRL-1 there are 2^{m-1}-1 free parameters and on CTRL-2 there is 1: the solver returns a
  family when a family is there, so the uniqueness on the torus is a measured result.
