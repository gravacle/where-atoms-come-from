  1. THE 'FREE WRITER' IS A TAUTOLOGY, NOT A DISCOVERY THAT COULD FAIL AT SCALE.  T1 measured
     dE = +0.000000 for the admissible writer on FOUR different states -- ground, middle, top,
     and a RANDOM pure state -- on all six carriers.  The one-line reason is exact and needs
     no computation: dE = Tr(rho W-dag H W) - Tr(rho H) and [W,H] = 0 gives W-dag H W = H.  The
     control on the same carriers, the cheapest NON-admissible flipper, returns +2, +12, +4,
     +6, +6 and +11.05.  So O-47's 'flips for free' is O-4's definition of ADMISSIBLE read back
     to us.  The question that has content is EXISTENCE, which is clause (iv).

  2. THE CRITERION IS A PAIR OF ANTICOMMUTING SYMMETRIES.  T2/T3: on all NINETEEN carriers,
     including the ones with NO records and the two controls, the count predicted from the
     symplectic form alone -- R commutes with H and SOME W commutes with H and anticommutes
     with R -- equals the count measured on the dense matrices, with no exceptions.  And when
     that pair exists, clauses (iii) and (iv) BOTH come free: (iv) because Tr(P_E R) = -Tr(P_E R),
     (iii) because a constant +-1 on a block cannot survive conjugation by W.

  3. CLAUSE (iv) IS NOT IMPLIED BY (i)-(iii).  The last column of T2 is non-zero on six
     carriers -- 6, 14 and 30 on the UNIFORM chain at n = 4,5,6, 56 on C2, 62 on C3, 62 on the
     field control.  T6 exhibits one inside the chain family itself: on the UNIFORM chain
     R = Z_0 Z_1 commutes with H and IS non-constant on an eigenspace, and max|Tr(P_E R)| runs
     2, 6, 18, 56, 180 at n = 4,6,8,10,12 -- it fails (iv), and fails it worse as n grows.  On
     the GENERIC chain the same operator fails (iii) instead.  Clause (iv) does real work.

  4. THE HYPOTHESIS AS HANDED TO THIS LANE, STRONG READING, IS FALSE.  T4: on the ZZ chain
     (both coupling sets), the 2D grid and the ZZZ chain, the number of records that can be
     flipped by an admissible operation FIXING THE OTHERS is 0 out of n, at every n tested.
     The controls in the same table return 4/4 for H = 0 and 2/4 and 3/6 for partially coupled
     chains, naming exactly the uncoupled sites -- so the zeros are measurements.  And the
     restriction to Paulis is not what is doing it: with generic couplings every eigenspace is
     EXACTLY 2-dimensional, the most general admissible flipping U was built explicitly with
     random phases, and it flips n/n records at n = 4,5,6,7 with ||[U,H]|| = 0.  NO admissible
     unitary of any kind flips one record alone on this carrier.

  5. VERDICT ON THE HYPOTHESIS.
     'H commutes with every record'            NECESSARY (it is clause (ii)), NOT sufficient:
                                               C2, C3 and the field control all satisfy it and
                                               have no records.
     'a symmetry flips each record INDIVIDUALLY' NEITHER necessary NOR sufficient.  Not
                                               necessary: false on every carrier where the
                                               construction works.  Not sufficient: H = 0
                                               satisfies it and carries no configuration
                                               energy at all.
     'a symmetry flips each record'            NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT given (i)+(ii) -- but
                                               it IS clause (iv) via C-11, so as a criterion it
                                               is a restatement, not an explanation.
     THE STATEMENT WITH CONTENT: H must admit A PAIR OF ANTICOMMUTING SYMMETRIES, one of which
     is the record.  Then (iii) and (iv) follow and the writer is free.  For a commuting-Pauli
     H with generic couplings this says exactly: the record is a nontrivial LOGICAL operator
     of the group generated by H's terms.  T5 shows commuting-Pauli is NEITHER necessary (the
     XY chain at n = 5 has 3 records) NOR sufficient (C2 and C3 have none).
