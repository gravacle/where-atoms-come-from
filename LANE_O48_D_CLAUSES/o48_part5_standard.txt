  READ -- PART 5, FILLED IN FROM THE NUMBERS ABOVE, NOT IN ADVANCE
====================================================================================================================

  (a) EXTENSIVE: YES, at a fixed coupling scale.  S(2n)/S(n) runs 2.333, 2.143, 2.067, 2.032,
      2.016, 2.008, 2.004 for uniform couplings and straddles 2 for random ones.  CAVEAT: this
      carrier has NO BATH, so the standard's 'at a FIXED ENVIRONMENT' clause is not tested here
      at all.  That is a weaker result than C-47's, not a stronger one.

  (b) ADDITIVE: YES, up to the bonds crossing the cut.  One crossing bond, defect/|E| falling
      0.333, 0.143, 0.067, 0.032, 0.016, 0.008 as n doubles.  INDUCED, not inserted.

  (c) NOT A COUNT: YES.  At fixed n and a fixed set of n records, changing only the couplings
      moves the eigenspace count from 6 to 32 and the ground energy from -5 to -31 at n = 6.
      It is also not topological -- it is a sum of local terms.  It clears C-35.

  (d) SIGN-DEFINITE: NO, AND THIS IS WHERE IT BREAKS.  C-46's ratio |sum|/sum|.| is 1.000000
      ONLY on the ground configuration.  Averaged over the record configurations at uniform
      couplings it runs 0.500, 0.375, 0.313, 0.273, 0.246, 0.226, 0.209, 0.196, 0.185 for
      n = 4..20 -- a clean n^(-1/2) decay (0.185 * sqrt(19) = 0.809, against sqrt(2/pi) =
      0.798).  That is C-46's screening signature: the configuration energy CANCELS MORE as
      records are added.  The GEN column plateaus at 0.500 instead, but that is one coupling
      of size 2^(n-2) dominating a sum of smaller ones -- a coupling-scale artifact (D-17),
      not accumulation.  A source must accumulate for an ARBITRARY configuration of matter,
      not only for the ground state, and this quantity does not.

  (e) SEPARATION-DEPENDENT POWER LAW: NO.  Two separate measurements, both negative.
      (e1) the record-record correlation |<Z_i Z_j>| is EXACTLY 1 at every separation from
           d = 1 to d = 11 and definite on every eigenspace -- perfect rigidity, no falloff of
           any kind, power law or otherwise.  That is long-range ORDER, not a force law.
      (e2) the cost of changing a pair correlation is FLAT at 2.000 for every d at uniform
           couplings.  With random couplings it appears to fall -- and the distribution
           control settles what that is: at the SAME mean coupling, narrowing the coupling
           distribution flattens the curve (399.79 -> 398.30 across d = 1..11 for J in
           [199,201], against 359.57 -> 61.80 for J in [1,400]).  The falloff is the ORDER
           STATISTICS OF THE INSERTED COUPLINGS, not an induced interaction.  NOTHING
           SEPARATION-DEPENDENT IS INDUCED BY THIS CARRIER.
      And the instrument is not blind: inserting K = 20 on the long bond (0,j) moves the
      measured cost from 2.000 to 40-42 at every j.  It registers a real long-range coupling
      when one is put in.

  VERDICT.  The configuration energy meets (a), (b) and (c), FAILS (d) for any configuration
  other than the ground state, and FAILS (e) outright.  It is a genuine interaction energy
  between records that costs no clause -- O-47's result stands and extends to every n -- but
  it is a CONTACT interaction that CANCELS, and on the two requirements that separate a source
  from a bookkeeping quantity it does not qualify.
