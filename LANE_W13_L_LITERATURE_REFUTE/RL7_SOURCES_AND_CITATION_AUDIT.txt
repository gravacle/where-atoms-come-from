C-1.  **THE TARGET QUOTES A 2010 OPEN PROBLEM AS THE STATE OF THE FIELD, AND THE PAPER THAT
      CLOSES IT IS IN THE TARGET'S OWN READING QUEUE, UNCITED.**
      THEOREMS_AND_CITATIONS.txt Sec. 0 heads its evidence block "AND THE AUTHORITY SAYS THE
      HARD PART IS HARD, IN SO MANY WORDS" and quotes LSV: "It is not known whether (1.3)
      holds for all cyclic actions."  LSV is 2009/2010.
      VESSELIN DIMITROV, arXiv:1611.04664 (v2, 1 Jan 2017), ANSWERS IT.  Read at the bytes:
        * abstract: "we prove a sub-Liouville bound ... on the distance from an N-torsion
          point of G_m^d to an algebraic subset" and "for all non-zero integer Laurent
          polynomials P in d commuting variables, we prove that the averages of log|P(x)|
          over zeta in mu_N^d, P(zeta) != 0, converge as N -> infinity to the Mahler measure";
        * introduction: "This addresses the problem left open in T. Ward's thesis and then in
          K. Schmidt's book, and the papers [63, 64] of D. Lind, K. Schmidt and E. Verbitskiy";
        * "In the present paper, we solve (B) for ALL Laurent polynomials P in the case that
          Gamma runs over the cubical sublattices N.Z^d";  "Theorem 1.  The hypotheses (A) and
          (B) are true for Gamma = N.Z^d, N -> infinity."
      CONSEQUENCES, EACH ONE A CORRECTION TO A TARGET FINDING:
        (a) The LSV "not known" sentence is NOT the field's current word and must not be
            printed as one.  It is nine years out of date on the cyclic case at Gamma = N Z^d.
        (b) Sec. 2.5's flat claim "NO theorem covers the case Z(P) infinite" is FALSE in the
            torsion-point setting: Dimitrov covers ALL Laurent polynomials, including the
            three zero-CIRCLE loci of M1_02 -- one of which is S1's own published ready state.
            L-5's parallel remark inherits the same correction.
        (c) L-9 rules that "the correct citation from that family is LIND-SCHMIDT-VERBITSKIY
            (2010)".  On the target's own criterion -- name the paper that actually treats the
            hard case -- the correct citation from that family in 2026 is DIMITROV 2017 (with
            LSV as the partial result it supersedes, and Habegger's Diophantine approximation
            to definable sets as the alternative route Dimitrov names).  L-9 convicts W-10 of
            "a second misattribution in the act of correcting the first" and then stops one
            paper short itself.
        (d) The recommended CITATION LIST for the note (Sec. 4, "CORE -- the note cannot be
            written without these") omits it.
      WHAT SURVIVES.  Dimitrov's averages are over TORSION points -- the corpus's FINITE-ORDER
      connections -- not over a single Z-orbit at an infinite-order connection.  So Dimitrov
      does NOT license N1, and the target's HEADLINE ("no published theorem licenses N1 as
      registered") is untouched.  What falls is the evidence the headline is resting on and

C-2.  **THE TARGET CITES THE WRONG BOYD 1981 FOR THE BOYD HEIGHT, AND ITS OWN PRIMARY SOURCE
      SAYS SO ON THE SAME PAGE IT QUOTES.**  The target's [2] is Boyd, "Speculations concerning
      the range of Mahler's measure", Canad. Math. Bull. 24 (1981) 453-469, and its Sec. 1.1
      quotes the Boyd height mu(r) from arXiv:2508.05910.  That paper attributes the classical
      Boyd-Lawton theorem to its [5] and [16], and defines the Boyd height with the pointer
      "(cp. [5, p. 118])" -- and its [5] is BOYD, "Kronecker's Theorem and Lehmer's Problem for
      Polynomials in Several Variables", J. Number Theory 13 (1981) 116-121, a DIFFERENT paper.
      Issa-Lalin (read at the bytes) cite the theorem as [Bo81a, Bo81b, La83], i.e. BOTH.
      So the target's [2] is not wrong, it is HALF: the paper that defines the quantity its own
      R1_08 A2 computes is missing from the list.  The target instructs "CITE LAWTON TWICE";
      BOYD ALSO NEEDS TWO ENTRIES.  Severity: low.  Verifiable: completely.
