--- 1.1  THE BOYD-LAWTON THEOREM (the one the corpus means, and the one that does NOT license N1)

Quoted from the introduction of "General Boyd-Lawton Theorems with Multivariable Limits"
(arXiv:2508.05910), read 2026-08-17:

    "m_n(P) = lim_{mu(r) -> infinity} m_1(P^(r))"
    "mu(r) = min{ ||v||_inf : v in Z^n, v != 0, and v . r = 0 }"

  with P^(r)(Z) = P(Z^{r_1},...,Z^{r_n}) and P a nonzero Laurent polynomial in
  C[Z_1^{+-1},...,Z_n^{+-1}], n >= 2.  NOTE THE COEFFICIENT FIELD: C, NOT Z.  Boyd (1981)
  conjectured it; Lawton (1983) proved it.
  mu(r) is the corpus's q(r) of R1_08 A2 (sealed) under a different name.

  THE OBJECT IT IS ABOUT.  P^(r) is the restriction of P to the ONE-PARAMETER SUBGROUP
  q_r : T -> T^n, z |-> (z^{r_1},...,z^{r_n}).  m_1(P^(r)) is the HAAR AVERAGE of log|P| over

  to the integral over T^n as the subgroups equidistribute.

  log-singular integrand.

--- 1.2  LAWTON'S OTHER THEOREM — THE SMALL-VALUE BOUND.  THIS ONE *IS* PART OF THE LICENCE.

Quoted VERBATIM from E. Dobrowolski, "A Note on Lawton's Theorem", Canad. Math. Bull. 60 (3)
(2017) 484-489, Section 1, where it is recalled as "Theorem 1 of Lawton's seminal paper".
This lane read that PDF at the bytes.

    "Let P(x) in C[x] be a monic polynomial and let k be the number of nonzero coefficients
     of P.  Then if k >= 2, there is a positive constant C_k that depends only on k such that
         mu{ x in [0,1) : |P(e^{2 pi i x})| < v } <= C_k v^{1/(k-1)}
     for every real v > 0."

  C[x].  COMPLEX COEFFICIENTS.  So it applies to this corpus's P on the WHOLE simplex,
  including the irrational ready states R1_08 A3 (sealed) correctly flagged as outside the
  integer-coefficient reading of Boyd-Lawton.  THAT GAP IS CLOSED HERE.

  Dobrowolski 2017 removes monicity (Thm 1.1, C_k = (k-1)(12 sqrt2/pi)^{(k-2)/(k-1)}) and
  supplies the SEVERAL-VARIABLE version this corpus needs (Thm 1.4, verbatim):

    "Let P(z_l) be a polynomial with complex coefficients.  If P has at least two monomials,
     then
        mu_l{ z in T^l : |P(z)| <= v } <= C(k_1,...,k_l) (v/h)^{1/(sum_{i=1}^{l}(k_i - 1))},
     where C(k_1,...,k_l) = C_{k_1} + ... + C_{k_l}"

  h = max modulus of the coefficients; k_i = number of terms of P read as a polynomial in z_i
  over the remaining variables.

  SPECIALISED TO N1's OWN POLYNOMIAL, computed here: P = p00 + p10 x + p01 y + p11 xy has
  k_1 = k_2 = 2, so sum(k_i - 1) = 2 and C = C_2 + C_2 = 2, giving for EVERY pi

        mu_2{ (x,y) in T^2 : |P| <= v }  <=  2 (v / max(pi))^{1/2}.            (*)

  (*) is checked in L4 on five ready states x four values of v: HOLDS on 20 of 20, worst
  (measured - bound) = -2.24e-02.  Measured true exponents: s ~ 2 at isolated simple zeros
  (K1's registered pi: 2.11), s ~ 1 on the zero-CIRCLE loci (S1's published ready state:
  0.94; four-class uniform: 0.88), mu = 0 identically when max(pi) > 1/2.
  (*) is not sharp.  ITS VALUE IS THAT ITS CONSTANT DOES NOT DEPEND ON pi, which is what a
  shell argument needs, because P's zero structure degenerates on three loci and a
  pi-dependent constant would not survive the limit.

--- 2.2  THE DIOPHANTINE INPUT THE FIELD USES

GELFOND, quoted verbatim from LSV Theorem 9.1 (their [4] = A. O. Gelfond, "Transcendental and
Algebraic Numbers", Dover 1960, Thm III):

    "Suppose that xi = (xi_1,...,xi_d) has algebraic coordinates that are multiplicatively
     independent.  Then for every epsilon > 0 there are only finitely many n in Z^d for which
     |xi^n - 1| < e^{-epsilon ||n||}, where ||n|| = max{|n_1|,...,|n_d|}."

LSV then say how they use it:

    "Now log|f| has only finitely many logarithmic singularities, and by Proposition 4.2 these
     all have ALGEBRAIC COORDINATES.  We can therefore use Gelfond's result to control the few
     potentially large negative values of log_0|f| for omega in Omega_Gamma near one of these
     singularities, to show that the Riemann sums in (9.1) will converge to the limit
     m(f) = h(alpha_{R_d/<f>})."
  And the limitation, which is also ours:
    "To make a similar argument work when U(f) is infinite, we would need an estimate of the
     form dist(U(f), omega) >= e^{-epsilon o(omega)} ... SUCH ESTIMATES, HOWEVER, DO NOT APPEAR
     TO BE AVAILABLE."

BAKER, the stronger (polynomial) form, which is what a SINGLE ORBIT needs where LSV's
finite-subgroup Riemann sums can get by with Gelfond's exponential form.
A. Baker, "Linear forms in the logarithms of algebraic numbers I-IV", Mathematika 13-15
(1966-1968); effective version A. Baker and G. Wustholz, "Logarithmic forms and group
varieties", J. reine angew. Math. 442 (1993) 19-62.  Statement as given on the Wikipedia
article "Baker's theorem", read 2026-08-17 (SECOND-HAND; the papers were not read):

    Baker 1966-67:  if lambda_1,...,lambda_n are logarithms of algebraic numbers, linearly
    independent over Q, then for algebraic beta_0,...,beta_n not all zero,
        |beta_0 + beta_1 lambda_1 + ... + beta_n lambda_n| > H^{-C},
    H the maximum height of the beta_i, C effectively computable.
    Baker-Wustholz 1993:  either Lambda = 0, or
        log|Lambda| > -C h(alpha_1)...h(alpha_n) log(max{|beta_1|,...,|beta_n|}),
        C = 18(n+1)! n^{n+1} (32 d)^{n+2} log(2 n d).

  THE POINT: Baker's bound is POLYNOMIAL in the coefficient size (H^{-C}), Gelfond's is
  EXPONENTIAL (e^{-eps||n||}).  A single Z-orbit visits ~N points in T^2 while the deepest
  admissible dip is at k ~ N, so an exponential bound leaves a residue of size ~eps N per
  visit and does not close; a polynomial bound leaves ~C log N and does.  THIS IS WHY THE
  LICENCE FOR N1 IS BAKER AND NOT GELFOND, EVEN THOUGH THE FIELD'S NEAREST WRITTEN ARGUMENT
  USES GELFOND.

--- 2.3  THEOREM L.  THE LICENCE, STATED.

  MULTIPLICATIVELY INDEPENDENT.  Assume the zero set Z(P) subset T^2 is finite and that
  (u^k, v^k) not in Z(P) for every k >= 1.  Then
        lim_{N -> infinity} (1/N) SUM_{k<=N} log|P(u^k, v^k)|  =  m(P).

         on T^2, P = 0 forces cos(arg x) = (p01^2+p11^2-p00^2-p10^2)/(2(p00 p10 - p01 p11)),

   (ii)  WEYL/KRONECKER on the continuous truncation max(log|P|, log eps) — free, no

   (iii) BAKER-WUSTHOLZ on the HOMOGENEOUS form gives |u^m v^n - 1| >> max(|m|,|n|)^{-C},

   (iv)  BAKER-WUSTHOLZ on the INHOMOGENEOUS form relative to each of the finitely many

   (v)   LAWTON 1983 Thm 1 / DOBROWOLSKI 2017 Thm 1.4 give the shell measure UNIFORMLY IN pi:
         mu_2{|P| <= w} <= 2 (w/max(pi))^{1/2}.
